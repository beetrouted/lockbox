Common questions

  • I forgot a vault's password. Nobody can reset it, not even us. That's what keeps it private. Vaults locked with Face ID or Touch ID need your Lockbox key instead: keep a backup of it (My vaults › Backup).
  • A new phone or Mac. Restore your key backup in People, then choose the same vaults folder (for example in iCloud Drive) in Settings.
  • Someone sent me a file. Save it first, then open it with Lockbox & Key, or in any browser if it ends in .html. A chat or mail preview can't open it.
  • Where are my vaults? Settings › Where vaults are kept. Their files have random names, so the folder shows nothing about what's inside.
  • Deleting the app. On an iPhone, deleting Lockbox & Key also deletes every vault kept in On My iPhone › Lockbox. Keep a backup of your iPhone, or keep your vaults in a folder in iCloud Drive. The app asks which you'd like when you keep your first vault, and you can change it in Settings › Where vaults are kept.
  • Earlier versions. When you save changes, the version before goes to the Trash on a Mac. On an iPhone it goes to Recently Deleted in the Files app, where Lockbox & Key keeps one earlier version of each vault for 30 days. Settings › Space shows the space they use and can remove them sooner.
  • Only Face ID, not the passcode. On an iPhone, touch and hold Lockbox & Key on the Home Screen, choose Require Face ID, and turn on Stolen Device Protection in Settings › Face ID & Passcode. Away from familiar places, your iPhone then opens the app only with Face ID.
  • How big can a vault be? As big as your free space. Lockbox & Key reads a vault a piece at a time, and videos of any size play inside the app without an unlocked copy. Very large pictures and PDFs are offered as a download instead.
  • Running out of space? Keep your vaults in iCloud Drive (Settings › Use iCloud Drive). Your iPhone can then keep vaults you aren't using only in iCloud, and downloads one when you open it. iCloud needs room for them too.
